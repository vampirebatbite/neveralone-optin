The Problem

14 million Americans fall every year.
Most can't get help when it happens.

The industry's answer has been watches, wearable pendants, and call centers.

Problems with today's solutions:

  • Most people forget or don't want to wear a monitor
  • WiFi-only devices go dark when the power goes out
  • Monthly fees of $50–$90, often locked into a multi-year contract
  • No product exists that can be placed near the floor, where people need it most

The Solution

Install it anywhere. Press it once. The people you trust are notified instantly.

NeverAlone is a wall-mounted button with WiFi and cellular connectivity. Press it and within seconds, a text, phone call, and push notification go to the people you've chosen: your family, your neighbors, your Circle. No wearable. No call center. No contract. No base station required.

The economics
  • Hardware is priced below cost on purpose. At 500-unit volume, landed hardware cost runs ~$108/unit. Selling it at $35–$59 is an intentional customer acquisition cost. At full price, the subscription's margin repays that loss in well under a year, and the account is profitable for the life of the subscription after that.
  • WiFi-primary, cellular backup keeps per-device variable costs low and margins strong even at small scale
  • Predictable, tiered pricing with no per-alert fees. Customers always know what they're paying — no surprise charges when someone presses the button.
Why customers stay
  • High emotional switching cost. Families trust the product with someone they love.
  • Multi-device installations deepen commitment across the household
  • Circle contacts pull family members into the ecosystem, compounding retention
  • Month-to-month keeps it honest. Customers stay because it works, not because they're trapped.

Questions and Answers

"Can't they just use their phone?"

In a fall scenario, the phone is usually in another room. NeverAlone is on the wall, reachable from the floor, and takes one press.

"What if the battery dies?"

Years of battery life by design. When it runs low, the whole Circle is alerted automatically. The device monitors itself so the family doesn't have to.

"What if they press it by accident?"

Every press triggers a 5-second countdown with an audible alert. Cancel in time and nothing is sent.

"What if the WiFi goes down?"

WiFi first, cellular backup. Automatic. And because it's battery-powered, it keeps working even if the power goes out entirely.

"Why the monthly fee?"

The subscription covers the SIM, network, and platform that keep every alert moving. It's what turns a button on a wall into something that actually works when it needs to.

"Is this a medical device?"

No. It's a notification tool, same category as a smart doorbell. Not FDA-regulated, which means no approval process, no compliance overhead, and a much faster path to market. However, it is likely FSA/HSA eligible, and may qualify under Medicaid HCBS provisions in some states.

"Why hasn't a big company built this already?"

Established PERS companies run on call center revenue. Routing to family instead would destroy their business model. They can see the gap and still can't fill it.

"What stops a competitor from copying this?"

A cellular-connected physical device with real-world installation and proven reliability takes time and capital to build. Every month of customer trust makes NeverAlone harder to displace.

"What if a Circle member misses the alert because their phone is on silent?"

Every NeverAlone account has a dedicated alert number. Circle members save it as a contact and turn on Emergency Bypass (iOS) or add it to Favorites (Android), the same setting used for calls you never want to miss. Setup is guided during onboarding. Even on silent, the alert rings through.

"What if someone tries to abuse the cellular connection?"

The WiFi-primary, cellular-backup architecture naturally limits exposure, and account-level usage monitoring is part of the plan to catch and address unusual patterns.

"Does it work everywhere?"

It depends on WiFi and cellular coverage at the install location, same as any connected device. Most of the US has strong coverage, though very rural or remote areas may have weaker signal. The app shows signal strength at setup so families know what to expect before they rely on it.

"Why pay a monthly fee when some competitors charge nothing ongoing?"

Some devices skip the monthly fee but require customers to source, purchase, and configure their own SIM card and data plan. NeverAlone includes fully managed cellular connectivity: no SIM to buy, no carrier to choose, no setup. It works out of the box and keeps working. The monthly fee is the price of that reliability.

"Is there a money-back guarantee?"

We're still working through the right structure. Connectivity carries real ongoing cost, so any guarantee needs to balance customer trust with that reality. More to come as this is finalized.

"Don't people who fall become incapacitated? How could they reach a button?"

Not every fall leaves someone completely immobile. A button within reach from the floor is accessible when standing isn't possible and a phone is out of reach. Placement is everything.
